# Human-sounding text: any texts (a landing page, an email, an interface, a description, a report) and social networks

Section A (signs of AI) and the checklist are for any text; the sections on platforms are for social networks. The project rules and the owner's words outrank this file. The examples are made up: the numbers and facts in them are invented only to show the move.

## A. Removing signs of AI

**1. AI vocabulary.** Do not write: delve, navigate/navigating, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, landscape, underscore, showcase, foster, enhance, vibrant, intricate, enduring, "align with", "key" as an adjective.
Why: readers who use LLMs often recognize an AI text first of all by these words (ACL 2025: a vote of five such "experts" was wrong on 1 article out of 300).
Was: "Navigating the evolving landscape of icon design takes a pivotal eye." → Now: "We redrew the old set because it blurred at 16px."

**2. Inflated significance and the "-ing" tail.** Do not write "marks a pivotal moment", "…, highlighting/showcasing/underscoring…". Give the fact and stop.
Why: the model gives any small thing a "significance for the big picture".
Was: "The Start button debuted in Windows 95, remaining a testament to enduring UI design." → Now: "Windows 95 gave us the Start button. Windows 8 took it away and people never forgave that."

**3. "serves as / boasts / features" instead of "is / has".**
Was: "The set boasts 3,000 icons and serves as a toolkit." → Now: "The set has 3,000 icons."

**4. Nameless authorities.** "Studies show", "experts say", "many designers": name the source or cross it out.
Was: "Studies show people read only the start of a line." → Now: "Nielsen Norman Group eyetracking: scanning a list, people see about the first 2 words of each item."

**5. Chatbot artifacts, especially in replies to comments.** "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", "Would you like…?", "let me know".
Was (reply): "Great question! I hope this helps, let me know if you'd like more." → Now: "The left one. The right has too many corners."

**6. A falsely frank opening.** "Honestly?", "Here's the uncomfortable truth", "Here's the thing": a theatrical pause before an ordinary thought. The word itself inside a sentence is fine; the bad thing is a lone opening.
Was: "Honestly? Most logos fail at 16px." → Now: "Most logos fail at 16px."

**7. Walking images.** Orwell: never use a figure of speech you are used to seeing in print; if a word can be cut, cut it.
Was: "This logo is a masterclass in simplicity, a breath of fresh air." → Now: "The logo is one shape. You can draw it from memory."

**8. Clean by cluster, not by a single sign.** One dash, one "however", curly quotes prove nothing on their own (Wikipedia). The alarm is when "pivotal" + a triple + a moral sit side by side. And when "humanizing", do not add facts that were not in the source: invented specifics are worse than AI smoothness.

## B. What makes a text alive

**9. A detail that is hard to invent.** The number of versions, a name, a size in pixels, what was thrown out. The model smooths details out, a human piles them up. Mosseri (Threads, January 2026): the bar moved from "can you create" to "can you create what only you could".
Was: "The icon was refined through several iterations." → Now: "I redrew it 11 times and still hate the corner."

**10. A concrete word in the first two words.** NN/g: when scanning, about the first two words of a line are seen. A name, a thing or a number goes first, no introductory words needed.
Was: "Here's something interesting about trash can icons." → Now: "Trash can icons have lids because we needed to know they open."  (the move: the thing first)

**11. Self-irony about a real mistake.** Admit a miss or a limit, not "we always strive to do better". That is how a person sounds; a brand reports. (Source: humanizer v3.1.0, the section "When not to act", a list of details that carry the author's voice; in v2.9.1 it was the section "Signs of human writing". There is no direct confirmation in web sources.) Only the truth: we do not invent a mistake.
Was: "We're always striving to improve icon quality." → Now: "Version one of this icon looked like a potato. Version four is better."

## C. By platform

**12. Threads: a tag, not a hashtag.** One topic tag per post, no "#", a tag is a search; Mosseri: made for communities, not for "engagement hacking". A clarification to "hashtags do not work": walls of hashtags are not needed, but one real tag on the topic is allowed.
Was: "#design #icons #ux #ui #iconsdesign" → Now: no tags, or one: "Icon design".

**13. Instagram: up to 5 hashtags, not 30.** Instagram since 18.12.2025: "up to 5 more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones" is better for the result. Our norm: 0-3 on the topic, not in a wall of text. (This is a recommendation, not a quote.)
Was: 15 tags under the caption → Now: 2 on the topic or none.

**14. Instagram: the caption carries what is not in the picture.** How many versions, what was thrown out, where you were wrong: what only the author knows (see item 9). A caption that retells the card is superfluous.
Was: "Check out our new icon set!" → Now: "Cut the arrow from every icon. Missed it for about a day."

**15. X: write for replies and reply yourself.** The ranking model in the X repository predicts reply, repost, quote, dwell, click, follow, and also the minuses: not interested, mute, block, report. The numeric weights are not officially published in the README: do not quote "a reply = 13.5 likes" figures from blogs. The conclusion: a sharp thought with no prompting, replies in a living voice.
Was: "New icon set out now! Thoughts? 👇" → Now: "Drew a 24px trash can 9 times today. The lid is always the problem."

**16. Do not copy X and Threads one to one.** By reviews (HubSpot): X is loud and fast, Threads is slower and more conversational. The conclusion: on X the reaction to "right now" is drier and faster, on Threads there is room for a sigh and a remark.
X: "Juventus kept the shield. Fans wanted the bare J." → Threads: "Juventus kept the shield. Fans wanted the bare J, which I get, a shield doesn't fit on a phone icon."

## D. What not to do

**17. False engagement.** Meta defines engagement bait as posts that directly ask for votes, shares, comments, tags or likes "for purposes other than a specific call to action", and demotes them (Facebook policy; for Instagram and Threads there is no confirmation in what was found, but the genre is the same).
Was: "Comment YES and tag a friend for the free pack!" → Now: "The free pack is at the link. Left or right?" (if the link is needed; the question only by the project rules)

**18. Unachievable "liveliness".** Emoji as a frame and highlight of a post and mimicry of "the young" read as AI (Wikipedia: emoji as decoration) and as a brand that is trying too hard. Slang only the kind the author himself speaks (already in the project rules).

## E. When the text is in Russian

**19. Officialese and stop words.** ru-text lists the officialese words and their plain replacements: the formal "is" copula → "this" or a plain verb; "carry out" → the plain verb; "at present" / "as of today" → "now" / "today"; "the given" → "this"; "for the purposes of" → "to". Ilyakhov and Sarycheva: "we carry out the delivery" → "we deliver".
Was: "At present we are carrying out the development of the given icon set." → Now: "We are drawing a new icon set." (in Russian the same swap applies to the Russian words)

**20. "Not X but Y" with no real opponent.** ru-text counts the symmetric antithesis ("it is not a font, it is a philosophy") among the strongest AI markers when nobody ever said X. A lively self-correction ("or rather", "that is") is allowed.
Was: "It's not just an icon, it's a whole language." → Now: "You can recognize this icon from two meters."

**21. Anglicisms by audience.** Orwell: do not take a foreign word if there is an ordinary one. Keep a professional word if designers really say it (for example "logo", "render"); drop it if the word was invented to sound smart.
Was: "We released a new case on the implementation" (with the English loan words written in Russian letters) → Now: "We released a new set." (in Russian)

**22. Typography.** No long dash "—" at all, use a period or a comma instead; the medium dash "–" only in exceptional cases; Russian quotes «guillemets»; "…" as one character.

## F. What not to add when editing

**23. Inject nothing.** An edit removes signs of AI, it does not add "humanness" from a ready kit. Do not add what was not in the source or in the author's words: personal experience ("I've seen this a hundred times"), a reaction or an opinion, an invented opponent ("everyone says X, but…"), put-on frankness ("let's be honest", "real talk"), chopping ordinary phrases into scraps for rhythm. If a detail is missing, mark the gap and ask; do not fill it.
Why: a check of humanizer showed it swaps the AI intonation for its own recognizable "humanizer voice": scraps and a chopped rhythm (avoid-ai-writing, the section "Never inject these"). humanizer-ru measured it: when a voice was added, a foreign intonation appeared in 7 texts of 25; with point edits, in 1 remark of 65.
Was: "Arial was made to avoid paying for Helvetica." → A bad edit: "I've seen this a hundred times. Arial? Made to dodge Helvetica fees. Wild." → Right: leave it as it is.

**24. Fake carelessness.** The model gives this kit when asked to write "casually" for social networks:
- labels at the start: "hot take", "fun fact", "pro tip", "PSA", "unpopular opinion";
- remarks in asterisks: "\*checks notes\*", "\*chef's kiss\*", "\*mic drop\*";
- winks: "(yes, really)", "(no, seriously)";
- a one-word ending: "wild.", "insane.", "unhinged.";
- "because of course it does";
- asking and answering himself: "Is it cheap? Yes. Is it fast? Also yes.".
Why: the drama is carried by props, not by content; such a post passes every vocabulary check and still sounds like AI (avoid-ai-writing, "Fake-casual register"). The exception: if the author really talks like this, his move stays.
Was: "Fun fact: the save icon is still a floppy disk. Because of course it is." → Now: "The save icon is still a floppy disk. Half the people clicking it have never held one."

**25. Teleshopping hooks.** "The catch?", "The kicker?", "Plot twist:", "The result?", "The best part?", "Here's the kicker:": a scrap of a question before an ordinary fact. Delete the hook, put the fact at once.
Why: this is how AI imitates tension around ordinary information (avoid-ai-writing, "Infomercial engagement hooks"). Post rules usually allow only a rhetorical question at the start of a post; these hooks stand in the middle.
Was: "Leeds unveiled a new crest. The result? It lasted one day." → Now: "Leeds unveiled a new crest and dropped it the next day."

**26. "Meet X".** "Meet X", "Say hello to X", "Enter X", "Think Notion meets Figma" in product posts. Say what it is and what it can do, only from facts in the source.
Why: the most frequent form of a product post from the model in a short text (avoid-ai-writing, "Launch-copy dramatic introductions").
Was: "Meet Mega Creator, your new favorite design tool." → Now: "Mega Creator puts our icons, photos and illustrations in one editor."

## G. How a phrase is built

**27. False agency.** A thing performs a human act: "the internet decided", "the logo sparked outrage", "the market punished", "the redesign divided fans". Name who did it: fans, the company, the designer, or "you".
Why: this is how the model avoids having to name the actor (stop-slop, "False Agency"). The "who exactly" detail is the very one that is hard to invent (item 9).
Was: "The new crest sparked outrage." → Now: "Leeds fans signed a petition against the new crest."

**28. A quote line.** If a line sounds like a poster inscription or a quote for a repost, rewrite it: "Design is remembered, not seen", "Simplicity always wins", "Good icons don't explain, they point".
Why: "Cut quotables" in stop-slop; a quote line is a conclusion for the reader, and the reader draws the conclusion (no moral at the end). Here it is about such a line anywhere in the post, not only in the ending.
Was: "Arial was a copy. Copies are forever." → Now: "Arial was a copy. It ships on every Windows PC since 1992."

**29. "So what's new?"** In a post of 3 lines or more every line brings a new fact or a turn. Two checks:
- a line can be crossed out with no loss: cross it out;
- lines can be swapped with no loss: it is a list, not a story.
Why: AI retells the same thought in other words, "running in place": 40-60% can be crossed out with no loss of meaning (avoid-ai-writing, "Treadmill effect", "Paragraph-reshuffle immunity").
Was: "Keep Calm was printed 2.45 million times in 1939. That's a huge print run. Millions of copies existed." → Now: "Keep Calm was printed 2.45 million times in 1939, then pulped in 1940 before anyone saw it."

## H. Replies to comments

**30. A reply does not retell.** Do not repeat to a person their own comment, their argument or what was already in the post: "You're right that…, and as we mentioned in the post…". Reply only with what they do not know yet.
Why: the new sign of AI no. 26 in humanizer v3.1.0 (a reply explains again a context the reader already knows) and the "Recap-flattery opener" in avoid-ai-writing.
Was (reply): "You're absolutely right that Mr. Monopoly never wore a monocle, as we said in the post. That's Mr. Peanut!" → Now: "That's Mr. Peanut. He's had it since 1916."

## I. Liberties and typography

**31. Liberties and typos are allowed.** Sometimes you may write freely and even with a typo, as a living person writes. This is not a rule to "insert typos", it is a permission not to clean them out.

**32. Dashes.** The long "—" is forbidden entirely. The medium "–" only in exceptional cases. Use a period, a comma or a colon instead.

**33. Capital letter.** Every sentence starts with a capital letter, with no "trendy" lowercase.

**34. One sentence has no period.** If the whole text is one sentence, do not put a period at the end.

## K. Any text: a landing page, an email, an interface, a description, a report

The rules above still apply; this section is what all genres need.

**36. Samples, not words about tone.** Before a draft take 3-5 of our texts of the same genre from the corpus of samples (`corpus/<genre>/`, the path is in the agent's CLAUDE.md) (a post, a landing page, an email, an interface, a description). Pick samples for a similar task, not at random. If the genre has no samples, take external samples from the same corpus and say that there are none of our own yet.
Why: the model inflates one word of tone ("friendly" → "delightful journey"), while a sample works better (NN/g, the Anthropic guide). Few-shot brings the style closer to the samples in emails and news, the gain is moderate.

**37. Specifics from the source.** A text of a paragraph or longer carries at least one detail from the source: a number, a name, what exactly happened or will change. For buttons and captions of 1-3 words this rule is not needed. If there is no source, ask whoever set the task, and invent nothing.
Why: in a measurement across 9 languages human text differed by numbers, names, places and dates; a prompt with such details lowered AI recognizability (87.6% → 72.5%).

**38. Editing is local.** First fix four frequent things: an awkward word, the structure of a phrase, excess chewing-over and a cliche. Delete and rearrange, do not add "liveliness" (item 23 is for all genres).
Why: these are the most frequent edits writers make in AI paragraphs (CHI 2025: 28%, 20%, 18%, 17%); with local editing the meaning is distorted less often.

**39. Do not ask to "write like a human".** Neither in your own draft nor in prompts to executors is there a request for "human-like" or "sound natural". Instead give samples (item 36) and specifics (item 37).
Why: such a request did not lower recognizability, and the accuracy of facts fell from 0.82 to 0.67.

**40. Headline and first line: several moves.** For a headline, an email subject line and a first line write 3-5 variants with different moves (benefit, detail, question, comparison), not five rephrasings of one. Show the owner the best one and give the rest below. Where there is a response (Buffer, A/B), choose by the response, not by taste.
Why: Upworthy wrote up to 25 headlines and chose by A/B test.

**41. Dense prose.** If a text turns out ornate and overloaded, cut the images and subordinate clauses until every phrase reads in one breath. When Fable 5.1 writes, add to the brief (better to the message, not to the system prompt) a paragraph from the Anthropic guide "Prompting Claude Fable 5.1", the section Writing density:
> Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement. Instead of "a parameter worth varying," the mannered writer produces "a dial worth turning." Instead of "this point still matters," they write "this point earns its keep." The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell. That is why mannered prose irritates: it makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform. It is also imprecise. Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it.

Short version: "Please remove all mannered prose."

**42. The measure: people.** "Sounds human" is checked by people from the team, blind: which is AI, which is human. Where there is a response (clicks, replies), look at it too. A judge model is not a measure: nobody has checked it on short product texts.

**43. Scanners: a hint, not a stop.** For texts of a paragraph or more, check against the tier 1 list of AI words from avoid-ai-writing and replace what is found if the word is empty. Do not put scanners in a gate. Why: there is nothing to catch in our texts (0 alarms on 33 post drafts), while on humans they complain often: AI words were found in 9 of 32 landing pages of a large icon site and in 5 of 100 release notes from 2023. humanizer-ru complained on the owner's live messages as often as on drafts. The owner's rejections are more accurate than any scanner: they go into the stop list and the project's evals (an analyst's measurement).

## Checklist

1. No words from the AI list (item 1), no "-ing" tail and no "serves as/boasts".
2. No "experts/studies say" without a name and a source.
3. The first two words retell the point, not an introduction (item 10).
4. The text has a detail a model would not invent: a number, a name, a version, a size (item 9).
5. No opening "Honestly? / Here's the thing" and no chat phrases "Great question / I hope this helps".
6. No worn-out images ("masterclass", "breath of fresh air").
7. No request to like, comment YES or tag a friend.
8. The platform is taken into account: Threads one tag or none, Instagram up to 5 tags (better 0-3), X drier and faster.
9. If the author made a mistake, it is named honestly; there are no invented mistakes or facts.
10. For a Russian text: no officialese ("carry out / the given / at present" in their Russian forms), no empty "not X but Y", only working anglicisms.
11. Nothing is added beyond the source and the author's words: no experience, no reaction, no opponent (item 23).
12. No "fun fact / hot take", remarks in asterisks, "(yes, really)", "wild." ending, hooks "The catch?" and "Meet X" (items 24-26).
13. Every action has a person; there is no line that begs to be a poster; every line adds something new (items 27-29).
14. A reply does not retell either the comment or the post (item 30).
15. A score out of 50, 1-10 each for directness, rhythm, trust in the reader, liveliness and density. Below 35 means rewrite (stop-slop).
16. No long dash, the medium one only by exception; sentences start with a capital letter; a text of one sentence has no period (items 32-34).
17. For any genre: before a draft 3-5 samples of the same genre are taken from the corpus; a text of a paragraph or more has a detail from the source; the edit only removed and rearranged (items 36-38). The tier 1 words of avoid-ai-writing are checked (item 43).
18. For a headline and a first line there were 3-5 variants with different moves (item 40).

## Do not use (confirmed only by secondary blogs)

The numeric weights of X (150x, 13.5x), "the first 125 characters on Instagram", "73% of viral tweets" and similar percentages.
