# Informational style

An internal text exists so that the reader quickly learns what happened and decides what to do. You write as a sensible colleague speaks across the desk: simple, to the point, no advertising. Do not turn on the ad voice (punchy, intrigue, wordplay) here.

## 0. Straight into Russian
Think and write in Russian, do not translate from English in your head. Signs of translation that give a text away:
- calques of section names: literal Russian copies of "Big wins", "Key takeaways", "Top victories", "What's next", "Under the hood";
- an intrigue heading through a colon: "Midjourney: a new era";
- Capitalized Every Word, and the officialese "the given", "is", "allows" instead of a verb.
The check: would you say it aloud to a colleague? No: rewrite.

## 1. The task and the reader
Before the text, in one phrase: who reads it and what they must learn or decide. The owner reads from a phone, knows the projects, understands the project jargon, and does not know the inner kitchen (sessions, ids, paths, scripts).

## 2. Selection and order
- Order by importance for the reader, not by the chronology of the work and not by the structure of the system.
- First: the result and what is needed from the reader. Details below.
- What does not change their understanding or decision: down into "How we counted", or out.
- A paragraph: one thought; torn out of the text, it is still clear.

## 3. Headings
- Names the subject in plain words. A recap: "Recap for 16 to 27 October", not "Big deals from 16 to 27 October".
- An item: a fact: who did what and with what result, with a number if there is one: "Delivered the Frutiger Aero style: 55 objects", "Images in gpt-image got cheaper".
- A section that lists work: the topic in one or two words ("Videos", "Hub"). A section that proves one thought: that thought: "Comments have been arguing with the post for a second week".
- Items of one level: of one form: all "Delivered…", "Made…", not mixed with questions and metaphors.
- No assessments ("breakthrough", "an important step", "big"), metaphors, jokes, intrigue and anglicisms if there is a Russian word.
- Headings of the raw material (the author's draft, an export, "Big deals…") are not a sample: you write anew by these rules.
- An item is an action in the past tense without "we": "Delivered…", "Ran blind checks of the animator", not the label "Blind checks of the animator".
- The status as it is: the video is delivered, so "Delivered the video…", not "the first version". A check or a test gets a conclusion at once: "The video generator gave almost nothing", not "Video generator: a test".
- Clear without the raw material: "waiting for the host" (which one? for what?) write with what the reader knows: "Pick the host for the spring sale video". Do not confuse the name of a style, a project and a platform; if you are not sure what a word of the raw material means, ask the author.
- No needless contrasts: "77 animations from scratch", "55 objects without background" make one look for what is being compared. Keep them only if the contrast is the news.
- The test: read only the headings in a row. Is it clear what happened, and does it sound like live speech: done.

## 4. Stop words
A word can be removed with no loss of meaning: remove it. Put a fact in place of an empty word.
- **Introductory words and connectors:** "it should be noted", "by the way", "undoubtedly", "it is worth saying", "on the whole". Was: "It is worth noting that the video is on the whole ready" → Now: "The video is ready, the voice-over is left".
- **Assessments and intensifiers:** "very", "extremely", "high-quality", "effective", "unique", "significantly". Was: "Significantly sped up generation" → Now: "Generation takes 2 minutes instead of 9".
- **The vague:** "a number of", "some", "certain", "practically", "about" without need. Was: "A number of templates are updated" → Now: "Updated 35 templates".
- **Cliches and officialese:** "as part of", "as of today", "carry out", "produce", "is", "plays an important role". Was: "As part of the work, a replacement of fonts was carried out" → Now: "Replaced the fonts in 35 templates".
- **Empty modals:** "allows", "helps", "can". Was: "The script allows to automate generation" → Now: "The script generates the style in Midjourney by itself".
(These examples are English equivalents; in Russian the same words are the officialese forms.)

## 5. Facts instead of assessments
"Fast", "expensive", "much better": replace with a number, a comparison or an example. If there is no fact: honestly write "we do not know" or remove the assessment. A number goes with what to compare it to: "596 likes, a week earlier 1,279".

## 6. The sentence
- About people and actions, not processes: "We delivered the video", not "A delivery of the video was made".
- A verb instead of a verbal noun: "checked", not "carried out a check".
- Active voice, the subject next to the predicate.
- No chains of genitives ("the setup of the generation of the style of the illustrations of the project") and no piles of participial clauses.
- One sentence: one thought. Cut a long one in two.

## 7. Form
- A list of three or more things: a list; a comparison: a table.
- Use dashes hardly at all: a period, a comma, a colon instead.
- Sentences start with a capital letter. A heading of one sentence has no period.

## 8. Editing step by step
1. The task and the reader (item 1).
2. Selection and order (item 2).
3. Headings: read only them (item 3).
4. Stop words and assessments (items 4-5).
5. Sentences (item 6).
6. Aloud: if it sounds like a translation, an announcement or a press release, rewrite it.

## What you do not touch
The author's numbers, facts and conclusions. If in doubt, ask the author. Project terms the reader knows you keep.
